Experience Proof-Based Thinking

Domain: Number Theory & Combinatorics
Problem: Show that among any $6$ integers, there exist two whose difference is divisible by $5$. Can you generalize this to $n$ integers for any divisor $d$?
Hint (Pigeonhole Principle): What are the possible remainders when an integer is divided by $5$? How many possible remainders exist, and how many numbers are we choosing?
Proof: By the Division Algorithm, every integer leaves a remainder in the set $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ modulo $5$. There are only $5$ distinct possible remainders ("pigeonholes"). If we choose $6$ integers ("pigeons"), by the Pigeonhole Principle, at least two numbers $a$ and $b$ must leave the exact same remainder $r$. Thus, $a \equiv b \pmod 5$, which implies $a - b$ is an exact multiple of $5$. Q.E.D.

Mathematical Rigor

The Four Olympiad Disciplines

Our curriculum does not follow school syllabi. It focuses on the four timeless branches of international competitive mathematics.

Number Theory

Integers, Primes & Structures

Exploration of fundamental divisibility, arithmetic structures, and modular behavior with emphasis on constructing non-trivial counterexamples and proofs.

Divisibility Modular Arithmetic Diophantine Equations Primes & Congruences Bézout & Euclidean Alg Constructions

Combinatorics

Discrete Structures & Logic

Mastering existence arguments, counting techniques, invariants, graph-based abstractions, and the art of constructing extremal configurations.

Pigeonhole Principle Invariants & Monovariants Extremal Principle Combinatorial Games Graph Theory Double Counting

Synthetic Geometry

Euclidean Proofs & Angles

Pure synthetic Euclidean geometry without coordinate crutches. Emphasizing cyclic configurations, harmonic bundles, transformations, and angle chasing.

Euclidean Geometry Angle Chasing Cyclic Quadrilaterals Power of a Point Transformations Auxiliary Constructions

Olympiad Algebra

Inequalities & Functions

Beyond mechanical manipulation: deep study of classical inequalities, functional equations over integers and reals, and polynomial roots.

AM-GM & Cauchy-Schwarz Functional Equations Polynomial Roots & Vieta Algebraic Manipulation Sequences & Recurrences

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about the screening, training, and selection.

What is PGMO?
Pakistan Girls Mathematical Olympiad (PGMO) is a national initiative designed to identify, train, mentor, and support girls interested in competitive mathematics and create a pathway toward international Olympiads.
What is OMG and how does it fit into PGMO?
Olympiad Math for Girls (OMG) was the original 2025 pilot training program. In the unified ecosystem, OMG now functions as the intensive proof-training camp stage where students master the core mathematical areas before the progressive PGMO selection rounds.
Is PGMO completely free?
Yes. Participation in the screening test, the OMG training camp, mock contests, and mentorship is 100% free. There is no tuition, examination fee, or paywall.
Do I need prior Olympiad experience?
Not at all. The nationwide screening test is specifically formulated to measure raw mathematical intuition, creative logic, and non-routine problem solving, rather than memorized competition formulas or expensive prior coaching.
Is PGMO only for students in Lahore, Karachi, and Islamabad?
No. One of our foundational missions is geographic accessibility. Scholars have joined us from Swat, Sukkur, Khanewal, Quetta, Peshawar, Daska, and remote towns across Pakistan. Classes and sessions are conducted online to ensure universal access.
Is this the official Pakistan IMO team?
PGMO works in collaboration alongside Pakistan's national IMO team selection committee to prepare and advance talented female competitors. Participation in official international competitions such as IMO and EGMO remains subject to national selection guidelines and approval.
